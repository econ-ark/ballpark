# Verification (Matsya vs. paper)

I accepted Matsya’s two-stage decomposition—**decision** then **shock realization**—because it matches the max-then-expectation structure in the household Bellman statement summarized in `ToramanSY_AlganRagot2009_Summary.ipynb` and keeps optimization separate from the Markov update on productivity. I edited the draft YAML to make explicit placeholders where the ballpark summary is thin: the borrowing limit (`a_min`) and the law of motion for next-period resources (`q_next` in the shock stage), both flagged as unresolved until checked against the published paper. I rejected treating the separable money-in-utility form in `dolo-plus-draft.yaml` as paper-identical: the summary’s Bellman excerpt records a **CES composite over consumption and money with leisure** (`arg2009-bellman-excerpt.md`), so the current YAML remains a **computational scaffold** until that utility block is aligned with Algan–Ragot (2009) or justified as a deliberate simplification after reading the PDF/`.mmd`.
