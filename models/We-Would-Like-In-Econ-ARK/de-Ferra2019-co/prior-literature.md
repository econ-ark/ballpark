# Prior Literature Summary: Household heterogeneity and the transmission of foreign shocks

## The papers my ballpark paper cites

De Ferra, Mitman & Romei (2019) sits at the intersection of two large literatures: heterogeneous-agent macroeconomics and open-economy monetary/exchange-rate policy. On the domestic side, the paper builds on the incomplete-markets tradition of Aiyagari (1994), Huggett (1993), and Imrohoroglu (1989), where households face uninsurable idiosyncratic income risk and borrowing constraints. A recent wave of work embedded this household heterogeneity into New Keynesian frameworks — notably Kaplan, Moll & Violante (2018) ("HANK"), Auclert (2019) on the redistribution channel of monetary policy, Gornemann, Kuester & Nakajima (2012), Lütticke (2015), and Bayer, Lütticke, Pham-Dao & Tjaden (2019) — showing that the distribution of wealth and income matters for how monetary policy transmits to aggregate demand.

On the open-economy side, the paper draws on the classic insight of Díaz-Alejandro (1963) and Krugman & Taylor (1978) that exchange-rate depreciations can be contractionary when they redistribute income from high- to low-spending agents. The standard small-open-economy New Keynesian framework of Galí & Monacelli (2005) and Obstfeld & Rogoff (2000) provides the benchmark setting, while Céspedes, Chang & Velasco (2004) and Aghion, Bacchetta & Banerjee (2004) introduced balance-sheet effects of exchange-rate movements. Schmitt-Grohé & Uribe (2016) studied how downward nominal wage rigidity interacts with currency pegs, and Calvo & Reinhart (2002) documented emerging-market "fear of floating." By combining heterogeneous-agent incomplete markets with an open-economy setting featuring foreign-currency debt, de Ferra, Mitman & Romei bridge these two literatures and provide a framework in which household balance-sheet heterogeneity shapes the macroeconomic consequences of foreign shocks and the optimal choice of exchange-rate regime.

## Key foundational papers

- **Aiyagari (1994)**: The workhorse incomplete-markets model with idiosyncratic earnings risk and borrowing constraints. Provides the foundation for the household side of the model.
- **Kaplan, Moll & Violante (2018)**: "Monetary Policy According to HANK" showed that household heterogeneity fundamentally alters monetary policy transmission, motivating the extension to an open-economy setting.
- **Auclert (2019)**: Formalized the redistribution channel of monetary policy, decomposing how interest-rate changes affect agents differently depending on their balance sheets — the theoretical lens de Ferra et al. apply to exchange-rate movements.
- **Díaz-Alejandro (1963) and Krugman & Taylor (1978)**: Classic papers on contractionary devaluations through redistribution — the core open-economy mechanism that de Ferra et al. microfound with heterogeneous agents.
- **Galí & Monacelli (2005)**: The standard small-open-economy New Keynesian model that provides the benchmark framework for analyzing exchange-rate regimes.

## Gap this paper addresses

Prior work showed that household heterogeneity matters for monetary policy (HANK) and that exchange-rate movements redistribute (Díaz-Alejandro), but no paper had combined the two to ask how the distribution of household balance sheets — especially foreign-currency debt positions — shapes the macroeconomic response to foreign shocks and the welfare ranking of exchange-rate regimes.
